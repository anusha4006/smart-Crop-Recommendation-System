How Sensorless Agriculture Works

A comprehensive guide on replacing physical IoT hardware with Meteorological APIs and Soil Taxonomy.

Why Eliminate Physical Sensors?
1. High Cost & Maintenance

Soil pH sensors and RS485 probes cost thousands of rupees. In real agricultural fields, pH glass electrodes degrade within 3-6 months due to soil mineralization and moisture variations.

2. Power & Connectivity Issues

IoT nodes require constant battery power or solar panels, microcontrollers (ESP32 / Arduino), and SIM GSM modules that suffer from weak cellular signal in rural farmland.

3. Extremely Localized Readings

A single moisture or pH probe samples only a few cubic centimeters of dirt, failing to represent an entire 2-to-5 acre farm with varying contours and drainage.

System Architecture Workflow
Step 1: Input

Farmer enters City/District name or adjusts sliders manually.

Step 2: Satellite APIs

Open-Meteo fetches real-time Temperature, Humidity & Rainfall via geocoding.

Step 3: Soil Taxonomy

Regional Soil Database matches soil type (Alluvial, Black, Red, etc.) and pH.

Step 4: Random Forest ML

Predicts optimal crop with 96.14% accuracy, confidence score & cultivation guide.

Random Forest is an ensemble of decision trees that handles non-linear agricultural boundaries exceptionally well. Unlike single Decision Trees, Random Forest resists overfitting via bootstrap aggregation (bagging) and feature bagging. It achieved 96.14% accuracy on the 4 sensorless features and 99.55% when soil nutrients were included, outperforming SVM and Naive Bayes.

Soil pH does not fluctuate rapidly from hour to hour; it is determined by regional parent geological rock and long-term weathering. Our system uses a curated agro-soil database containing known soil classifications (Alluvial soil ~7.0 pH, Black Regur soil ~7.6 pH, Red soil ~6.2 pH, Laterite soil ~5.2 pH, Arid desert soil ~8.0 pH). When a farmer enters their district or selects their soil type, the baseline pH is mapped accurately without needing vulnerable glass electrodes.
